# BitMine Immersion Technologies (BMNR / BMNP) – Investment Investigation Summary

**Date compiled:** November 2026  
**Purpose:** Structured reference for future review of BitMine as a potential investment. Covers company model, finances, MAVAN, risks, scenarios, and long-term considerations based on SEC filings, company disclosures, and analysis conducted through early November 2026.

---

## 1. Company Overview

BitMine Immersion Technologies (NYSE: BMNR) is a publicly listed company that has transformed into a pure-play Ethereum treasury vehicle. Its primary strategy is the accumulation of ETH as a long-term reserve asset, combined with staking to generate yield.

The company previously had Bitcoin mining and related activities but has largely pivoted away from proprietary mining toward ETH treasury management and institutional staking infrastructure.

Key identifiers:
- Common stock: BMNR
- Series A Perpetual Preferred Stock: BMNP
- CIK: 0001829311

As of early November 2026, BitMine held approximately 6.06 million ETH (≈ 4.9% of stated circulating supply) and had staked roughly 5.23 million ETH (≈ 86% of its holdings).

---

## 2. Business Model – How the Company Makes Money

BitMine’s core model has two layers:

1. **ETH Treasury**  
   Accumulates ETH on the balance sheet, primarily funded by equity issuance (ATM offerings and other capital raises). The long-term target has been described as the “Alchemy of 5%” — reaching ownership of 5% of Ethereum’s supply.

2. **Staking Yield via MAVAN**  
   The large majority of ETH is staked to earn protocol rewards. Staking is the dominant source of operating revenue (reported as high as ~98% of recent quarterly revenue in some periods).

Revenue is therefore driven by:
- Quantity of ETH staked
- Ethereum staking yield (historically in the low-to-mid 2% range in recent periods; company has referenced figures around 2.5–3.1% depending on the measurement window)
- ETH price (affects both the value of the treasury and the dollar value of rewards)

The company has indicated that once the 5% accumulation target is reached, the focus would shift more toward monetizing the existing large position through staking and related services rather than continued aggressive accumulation.

---

## 3. MAVAN Platform

MAVAN (Made in America Validator Network) is BitMine’s proprietary institutional-grade Ethereum staking and validation platform.

- Originally built to stake BitMine’s own large ETH holdings.
- Positioned as a U.S.-based infrastructure option for institutions that prefer domestic validation, while also supporting a globally distributed architecture.
- Opened to third-party institutional investors, custodians, and ecosystem partners.
- Generates staking rewards on BitMine’s own ETH and potential service fees on third-party assets under management.

At scale (full staking of BitMine’s ETH plus partners), management has projected annualized staking rewards in the $350–415 million range depending on yield assumptions and the portion of ETH deployed.

MAVAN is a validator/operator business, not a Layer-2 sequencer, shared sequencing service, or transaction-processing rails business. It earns yield on staked ETH rather than a take-rate on transaction volume.

## 4. Capital Structure

### Common Stock (BMNR)
- Significant historical dilution through ATM offerings and equity raises used to fund ETH purchases.
- Share count grew substantially (from hundreds of millions into the ~600 million range by mid-2026 filings).
- Dilution risk has been a primary concern for common equity holders. Management commentary around reaching the 5% ETH target has been interpreted by some as potentially reducing the need for ongoing large-scale equity issuance, though this remains execution-dependent.

### Series A Perpetual Preferred Stock (BMNP)
- 3.5 million shares issued (June 2026 offering).
- Stated amount / liquidation preference: $100 per share.
- Fixed cumulative dividend: 9.50% per annum on the $100 stated amount → **$33.25 million annual cash obligation**.
- Dividends paid weekly when declared.
- Cumulative: unpaid amounts accrue and compound at an escalating rate (starting at the base rate plus 5 bps, stepping up, capped at 15% per annum).
- Ranking: senior to common stock for dividends and liquidation.
- Redemption (issuer call):
  - First ~18 months: 110% of stated amount + unpaid dividends
  - Next period to ~3 years: 105%
  - Thereafter: 100%
- Holders have certain governance rights if dividends remain unpaid for defined periods (including potential board seats).
- The preferred creates a fixed senior cash claim that must be serviced regardless of ETH price or staking performance.

The preferred cannot simply be ignored. Non-payment leads to compounding arrears, blocks common dividends, and can trigger preferred-holder governance rights. The company is also obligated under the terms to use commercially reasonable efforts to raise capital (including by selling assets or securities) to cover deferred dividends.

## 6. Operating Expenses

Three independent approaches were used:

**From company filings**  
- Most recent clean quarterly G&A (quarter ended 31 May 2026): ≈ $37.3 million  
- Earlier quarters in fiscal 2026 were much higher due to one-time capital-raising, advisory, legal, and setup costs  
- Company guidance referenced ongoing digital-asset management fees in the $40–50 million annual range plus public-company costs

**Bottom-up estimation (nature of business)**  
Very small headcount (reports in the single-digit to low-teens range), combined with the costs of running a large institutional validator set, custody/security, compliance, and public-company overhead. Reasonable range: $80–160 million annually.

**Peer / industry context**  
Pure institutional staking operators carry meaningful infrastructure, engineering, and compliance costs at scale. BitMine’s combination of massive own-ETH staking plus public-company status places it in a higher cost band than a pure private staking provider but lower than a full exchange or large traditional miner.

**Working estimate used for analysis**: **$120–150 million per year** in ongoing operating expenses (normalized, post-setup phase).

- In a prolonged deep bear, the company would need to draw on cash reserves, sell ETH, raise capital, or manage preferred payments carefully.

## 8. Risk Factors and Red Flags

- **Dilution**: Historical heavy share issuance to fund ETH accumulation. Future issuance risk depends on whether the 5% target truly reduces the need for new equity.
- **ETH price and yield volatility**: Both the balance-sheet value and the cash generation from staking move with the crypto cycle.
- **Fixed preferred claim**: $33.25 million annual senior obligation that compounds if unpaid.
- **Operating cost base**: Still being normalized after a period of heavy one-time expenses.
- **Concentration**: Extremely high exposure to a single asset (ETH) and to staking economics.
- **Execution on third-party AUM**: MAVAN’s ability to attract and retain meaningful external assets under management remains to be proven at scale and with transparent disclosure.
- **Governance / preferred rights**: Prolonged non-payment of preferred dividends can shift board influence.

Positive structural points:
- Very low traditional debt
- Meaningful cash buffer relative to the preferred obligation
- High percentage of ETH already staked
- Transparent (if volatile) mark-to-market accounting for the treasury

## 9. Long-Term Risk / Reward View (≈ 10-year horizon)

**BMNR (common)**  
- Levered exposure to ETH price appreciation plus staking yield.
- Upside can be substantial if ETH performs well over a full cycle and dilution moderates.
- Downside is severe in a multi-year crypto winter because of the combination of asset-price decline, lower yields, ongoing costs, and the senior preferred claim.
- Fits as a high-conviction, high-volatility satellite position for an investor who already has a resilient core portfolio and wants deliberate crypto exposure (e.g., 2–3% of total portfolio).

**BMNP (preferred)**  
- Fixed 9.5% coupon on $100 stated value, paid weekly when declared.
- Senior claim relative to common.
- Credit risk is ultimately tied to the company’s ability to generate enough cash (or raise capital / sell assets) to service the dividend through cycles.
- More bond-like than the common, but still crypto-cycle dependent because the underlying cash engine is ETH staking.
- Attractive only if the investor is comfortable with the residual crypto and execution risks and values the senior claim and high current yield.

Neither security is a low-risk “yield play.” Both are expressions of a concentrated ETH treasury + staking strategy.

## 10. Portfolio Fit Considerations

BitMine provides leveraged, equity-wrapped exposure to Ethereum (price + staking yield) through a public vehicle.  

It is **not** a pure transaction-volume or L2-rails business. It does not match the profile of a company that earns a take-rate on high volumes of Layer-2 activity while avoiding long-term custody of the underlying assets.

For an investor seeking:
- Direct or leveraged ETH exposure with some yield → BitMine can serve that role (with full awareness of dilution, preferred seniority, and cycle risk).
- Exposure to L2 sequencing, shared sequencing, interoperability, or payment rails without balance-sheet asset risk → BitMine is not the appropriate vehicle.

## 12. Summary Assessment

BitMine is a high-conviction Ethereum treasury and staking company. Its economics are dominated by the size of its ETH holdings, the percentage staked, the prevailing staking yield, and the ETH price. The preferred stock adds a fixed senior cash claim that improves the risk profile for preferred holders relative to common but does not eliminate cycle risk.

The model can generate substantial cash flow in constructive ETH environments and faces material pressure in deep or prolonged bear markets. Dilution history and the still-evolving operating cost base remain important variables.
